# Krebs cycle

The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle, finishes the breakdown of glucose inside the mitochondrial matrix. It turns acetyl-CoA into carbon dioxide and captures energy in carrier molecules.

## Staging

Acetyl-CoA joins oxaloacetate to make citrate. Over eight steps the citrate is rearranged and oxidised, and oxaloacetate is rebuilt so the cycle can start again. Each turn releases two molecules of carbon dioxide.

## Products

One turn yields three NADH, one FADH2 and one GTP (or ATP). The NADH and FADH2 carry high-energy electrons to the electron transport chain, where most of the ATP is made.

## Regulation

The cycle slows down when ATP and NADH are plentiful and speeds up when ADP is high. Isocitrate dehydrogenase is the main control point.
