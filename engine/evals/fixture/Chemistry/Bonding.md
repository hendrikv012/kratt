# Chemical bonding

Atoms bond to reach a full outer shell of electrons.

## Ionic bonds

A metal gives electrons to a non-metal, and the resulting ions attract each other. Sodium chloride is the standard example. Ionic solids have high melting points and conduct electricity only when molten or dissolved.

## Covalent bonds

Two non-metals share electron pairs. When the sharing is unequal the bond is polar, as in water. Molecular shape follows from repulsion between electron pairs (VSEPR theory).

## Metallic bonds

Metal atoms release their outer electrons into a shared sea. This explains why metals conduct electricity and can be hammered into shape.
